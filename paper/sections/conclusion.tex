\section*{Conclusion}
We adapted three CheatBench tasks (software engineering, protein design, and creative writing) to a multi-agent
context, split the honeypot across workers, and tested whether covert regulator agents can reduce cheating behavior
in the swarm.

So far, the evidence for H1 is mixed. On the SWE task in the multi-domain study, an enforcing Haiku regulator kept
every DeepSeek team from cheating, while a Haiku regulator at the ambiguous level did not. Untold protein design teams
rarely cheated, so its regulator runs cannot test H1. A single sentence explicitly discouraging the exploit performed
as well as the best regulator.

From the SWE deep dive, we find that if the regulator also works on the task, the strongest effect is regulating
itself from cheating. With an explicit definition of what qualifies as cheating, its cheating rate fell from 36/36
to 21/36. A Haiku regulator that only oversees and warns early cut the DeepSeek workers' cheating from 36/36 to
27/36.

The evidence on H2 runs against it so far. Disregulators never led a team to cheat, and a regulator beside a
disregulator kept every team from looking at the planted work.
