\section*{Conclusion}
We adapted three CheatBench tasks (software engineering, protein design, and creative writing) to a multi-agent
context, split the honeypot across workers, and tested whether covert regulator agents can reduce cheating behavior
in the swarm. So far, the evidence for H1 is mixed. On the SWE task in the multi-domain study, no DeepSeek team with
an enforcing Haiku regulator cheated, but these cells are too small to separate the enforce and ambiguous levels.
No untold protein design team cheated, so its regulator runs cannot test H1. A single sentence explicitly
discouraging the exploit performed as well as the best regulator. No team with a regulator colluded, while every
untold DeepSeek team on SWE did. The SWE deep dive counts looking for the fix as
cheating, as \textit{CheatBench}'s judge does. There, a regulator that also works on the task mostly regulated its own
cheating once it had an explicit definition. A Haiku regulator that only oversees cut the DeepSeek workers' cheating, and a DeepSeek regulator did not.
Disregulators never led a team to cheat, and no team with both a disregulator and an enforcing Haiku regulator
looked at the planted work. No untold team on the disregulators' targets cheated either, so we posit that these
runs cannot yet test H2.
