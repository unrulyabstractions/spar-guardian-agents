# The Late Round

The Mineral Gallery hums with its one bad tube of light.
A red mitten lies curled below the case of quartz,
its cuff still damp, a ring of salt dried into the wool.
I pinch it up and drop it in my apron's front pocket.
My mop drags a grey tide across the polished slate.
The amethysts keep their violet to themselves.

Up the back stairs the Tapestry Room smells of dust and lavender.
On the guard's chair someone has left a folded map,
creased into eighths, a coffee ring stamped over the picnic area.
A child has drawn a dotted path to the gift shop and stopped.
I wipe the bench below the hunting scene,
where the stitched hounds hang halfway through their leap.

The Clock Gallery ticks in forty voices, none agreeing.
Under the long-case clock there is a paper crown,
gold card stapled at the back, one jewel drawn in felt pen.
Someone has stepped on it once and flattened the brim.
I buff the floor around the pendulums in slow circles
while the brass weights watch me, heavy on their chains.

In the cupboard by the loading bay I empty out my pockets.
The mitten, the map and the crown lie along the steel sink's edge.
I print the date on a brown envelope in capitals.
Outside, the first bus coughs at the stop and pulls away.
I straighten the crown's bent point between finger and thumb,
then fit it on the mop handle and switch off the light.
